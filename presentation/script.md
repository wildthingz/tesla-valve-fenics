- Hello my name is Hatef and I'm going to present to you the research Daniele Rizzi and myself conducted on Telsa microvalves for high performance finite element method course on Edx
- Tesla microvalve is a type of microfluidic valve. In the recent years the use of microfluidics has gained a lot of traction. Microfluidics are widely employed in areas from biomedical and drug delivery to space and fuel cell systems. You can see an example of a micorvalve in the image below.
- Tesla valve, named after its inventor, is a passive valve which allows flow in one direction while prevents the flow in the other direction. The efficiency of a tesla microvalve is calculated through it's diodicty.
- In order to analyze the flow in a tesla valve we considered two different designs. We designed these following the standard guidlines for Tesla valves. We used OnShape as our main design tool.
- Afterwards, we exported the designs as .step files and used GmSh and meshio to mesh them.
- In fenics we setup a 3D navier-stokes problem using Taylor-Hood elements. We applied a stablization method to reduce numerical instabilities and used crank-nicholson method to facilitate the convergence. As boundary condition we assumed the walls to be no slip. We assigned an inlet velocity with a parabaloid profile and we set the outlet pressure to 0 Pa. Finally we used material properties of water in out simulations. 
- We used three flow cases with different inlet velocities for each of the designs.
- As it can be seen by comparing the velocity profiles of forward (figures on the right) and reverse flow (figures on the left), in all the simulated cases velocity fields of forward and reverse flow are significantly different. In the forward flow simulations, the fluid inside the Tesla microvalve is accelerated from the inlet to the outlet. Some of the fluid goes into the side channel when reaching the T-junction which contributes to a reduction in the diodicity. On the other hand, in the reverse flow simulations the fluid is again accelerated at the inlet but it is split in the main and side channel when reaching the Y junction. The main and side flows then continue until they reach the T-junction, when the side flow is re-injected in the main channel. This stage is very important in the evaluation of the diodicity of the valve; when the side flow is injected in the main one, it contributes to obstacle the main flow and generates an increase in the pressure drop between the inlet and the outlet of the valve. Due to the definition of the diodicity as the ratio between the pressure drop of the reverse flow over the pressure drop of the forward flow, when the reverse pressure drop increases, the diodicity increases too.
- When the inlet velocity increases, a higher volume of fluid is drained into the side channel with respect to the main channel; in this condition, when the side flow is re-injected in the main channel, it opposes to the main flow with the effect of increasing the pressure drop which causes an increase in the diodicity.
- The geometry of the Tesla microvalve has an important effect on the diodicity too. As one can imagine, when the angle of the T-junction is increased, the side flow is expected to be more opposed to the main flow causing higher reverse pressure drops in Design B with respect to Design A.However, as reported in table 1, we found that the diodicity values of Design A and B are the same in the case of inlet velocity 0.3m/s, and at inlet velocities 1.5m/s and 5.0m/s they are even lower in the case of Design B with respect to Design A. This unexpected result could be explained by the presence of flaws in the model implemented in the simulation, which does not take into account capillarity, a physical effect important when studying the behavior of fluids in micro channels, or flaws in the design that should be studied deeper by evaluating the diodicity in other Tesla valve designs.
- In conclusion, behaviour of a Tesla microvalve was simulated. Impact of inlet velocity and different Tesla valve design were evaluated. It's been found that the diodicity of Tesla valve increases with the inlet velocity. However, the effect of the different Tesla valve geometry results in unexpected diodicity values.
